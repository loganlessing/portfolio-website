Overview

At MSU Formula Racing I was responsible for the design and packaging of the EV battery pack structure — a core component of the car that sits at the intersection of mechanical integrity, electrical safety, and manufacturing reality. The project ran from initial concept through to manufactured and validated parts, giving me end-to-end ownership of a safety-critical system.

The Challenge

Packaging a high-voltage accumulator into a race car chassis — safely, lightly, and within a tight timeline.

Formula SAE rules impose strict requirements on accumulator containment structures, deformation zones, and electrical isolation. At the same time, every gram of structure weight directly hurts lap time. The challenge was designing a structure that passed all regulatory requirements while being manufacturable by a student team with limited tooling.

Process
01
Concept & Packaging

Worked through multiple packaging layouts in NX CAD to determine how the cell modules, BMS, and cooling system could fit within the chassis envelope while keeping the center of gravity low.

02
Structural Analysis

Used ANSYS FEA to validate the enclosure against SAE-defined impact load cases. Iterated on wall thickness, gusset placement, and material selection until all cases passed with an acceptable safety factor.

03
DFM & Manufacturing

Adapted designs in Fusion 360 for student-team fabrication — waterjet cutting, bending, and TIG welding. Documented the full process in a manufacturing package tracked via GitHub.

Results

Passed all SAE structural requirements. Part manufactured and installed in the 2024 competition vehicle.

The final design cleared all required load cases in FEA with a minimum safety factor of 2.1 across the governing scenarios. Weight came in under target, and the manufacturing package was completed on schedule — the structure was fabricated in-house and installed in the 2024 FSAE EV competition car.

More than the technical outcome, the project built my understanding of how to take full ownership of a system — from requirements and constraints through to a part in your hands — and how to communicate design decisions clearly across mechanical, electrical, and manufacturing stakeholders.
