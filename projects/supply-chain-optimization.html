Supply Chain Optimization

2024

Cover image — replace with images/supply-chain/cover.jpg

Problem

A regional distribution company was experiencing significant lead time variability and excess inventory holding costs due to misaligned ordering policies across their supplier network. The lack of visibility between tiers created cascading delays that compounded at the point of fulfillment.

The goal was to identify the root causes driving variability and redesign the ordering and replenishment policies to reduce total cost while maintaining service level targets.

Process

I began with a demand data audit across 18 months of transaction records, mapping the variance patterns by SKU category and supplier. This revealed a clear bullwhip effect — small demand fluctuations at retail were amplified 3–4x by the time orders reached Tier 2 suppliers.

I built a simulation model in Arena to test alternative replenishment policies — comparing periodic review, continuous review, and a hybrid vendor-managed inventory approach. Scenarios were run against historical demand distributions with Monte Carlo sampling to stress-test edge cases.

Outcome

The recommended hybrid VMI policy reduced average inventory holding cost by 22% while improving on-time delivery rates from 87% to 94%. The simulation output also identified two supplier relationships that were structural bottlenecks — flagged for renegotiation in the following procurement cycle.

The client implemented the new policy across their top 40 SKUs in Q3, with full network rollout planned for the following year.
