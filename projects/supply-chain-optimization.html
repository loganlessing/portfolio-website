Overview

A regional distribution company was struggling with lead time variability and excess inventory costs caused by misaligned ordering policies across their supplier network. This project redesigned their replenishment strategy using simulation modeling and demand analysis to reduce total cost while maintaining service levels.

The Challenge

Taming a bullwhip effect that was amplifying demand 3–4x across supplier tiers.

The lack of visibility between supply chain tiers was creating a classic bullwhip effect — small fluctuations in retail demand were amplifying 3–4x by the time orders reached Tier 2 suppliers. This drove up safety stock requirements and created recurring stockouts on high-velocity SKUs.

Process
01
Demand Data Audit

Started with an 18-month demand data audit, mapping variance patterns by SKU category and supplier using Excel / VBA. This confirmed the bullwhip effect and isolated the two supplier relationships causing the most amplification.

02
Simulation Modeling

Built a discrete-event simulation in Arena to test three alternative replenishment policies — periodic review, continuous review, and a hybrid vendor-managed inventory approach — stress-tested against historical demand with Monte Carlo sampling.

03
Policy Evaluation

Compared each policy on the cost-versus-service-level tradeoff and identified two structural bottleneck suppliers, recommending the hybrid VMI policy for rollout across the highest-velocity SKUs.

Results

Cut average inventory holding cost by 22% while lifting on-time delivery from 87% to 94%.

The recommended hybrid VMI policy reduced average inventory holding cost by 22% while improving on-time delivery from 87% to 94%. The simulation also identified two structural bottleneck suppliers — flagged for renegotiation in the next procurement cycle.

The client rolled out the new policy across their top 40 SKUs in Q3, with full network implementation planned for the following year. A future phase would integrate real-time POS feeds and a machine-learning demand-forecasting layer to further reduce safety stock.
