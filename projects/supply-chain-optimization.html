Supply Chain Optimization

Overview

A regional distribution company was struggling with lead time variability and excess inventory costs caused by misaligned ordering policies across their supplier network. This project redesigned their replenishment strategy using simulation modeling and demand analysis to reduce total cost while maintaining service levels.

Problem Statement

The lack of visibility between supply chain tiers was creating a classic bullwhip effect — small fluctuations in retail demand were amplifying 3–4x by the time orders reached Tier 2 suppliers. This drove up safety stock requirements and created recurring stockouts on high-velocity SKUs.

Design & Process

I started with an 18-month demand data audit, mapping variance patterns by SKU category and supplier. This confirmed the bullwhip effect and isolated the two supplier relationships causing the most amplification.

I then built a discrete-event simulation in Arena to test three alternative replenishment policies: periodic review, continuous review, and a hybrid vendor-managed inventory approach. Scenarios ran against historical demand distributions with Monte Carlo sampling to stress-test edge cases.

Results

The recommended hybrid VMI policy reduced average inventory holding cost by 22% while improving on-time delivery from 87% to 94%. The simulation also identified two structural bottleneck suppliers — flagged for renegotiation in the next procurement cycle.

The client rolled out the new policy across their top 40 SKUs in Q3, with full network implementation planned for the following year.

Future Steps

The next phase would integrate real-time POS data feeds into the replenishment triggers, reducing the lag between retail demand signals and upstream ordering. A machine learning demand forecasting layer would further reduce safety stock requirements.
